Science

The quiet science of attention: why your eyes move before you do

By Maya Lindqvist · 9 min read

Every second, your eyes make three or four rapid jumps called saccades. Between them they rest for a few hundred milliseconds, and that pause is where reading actually happens.

Researchers have long known that where we look reveals what we're about to do. A glance toward the bottom of a page is, more often than not, a request for more.

That small, unconscious signal turns out to be surprisingly easy to read with an ordinary webcam.

When the signal is clear, the page simply follows.
